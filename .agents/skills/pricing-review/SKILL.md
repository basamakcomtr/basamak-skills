# Pricing Review

## Objective
Recommend a **defensible pricing decision**, not a psychologically attractive number without evidence. Distinguish list price, billed price, taxes, discounts and renewal terms.

## Procedure
1. Capture current offer: audience, billing unit (seat, organization, usage), currency, monthly/annual term, included features, support limits, variable cost and known gross margin. Mark unknowns explicitly.
2. Identify the value metric closest to customer benefit. Compare at most **two pricing structures** (e.g. flat annual subscription vs tiered usage). Note abuse/overage risks, buyer comprehension and revenue predictability.
3. If external research is available, record dated competitor packages and what they actually include. Do not treat a promotional price as standard or assume rival economics are known.
4. Stress-test three customer situations: lightweight user, typical customer, and heavy or growing customer. Quantify only from supplied or verified inputs; otherwise use qualitative ranges.
5. Evaluate existing-customer fairness: notice, contract commitments, invoice clarity, feature continuity, cancellation and upgrade/downgrade behavior. Any legal/tax or regulatory detail requiring jurisdiction-specific advice must be marked for professional verification.
6. Decide the **smallest recommended change** with clear reasoning. State what data would overturn it. If no solid inputs are available, supply a decision framework rather than a made-up price.

## Output
Current situation and evidence; value metric; two-option comparison; recommended packaging/price logic; customer transition risk; 2–4 measurable validation signals; outstanding data.

## Guardrails
Never manufacture willingness-to-pay, churn impact, CAC, LTV, competitor prices, inflation figures or exact tax treatment. No live price change, contract edit, billing action or promotional claim without authorization. Related: `saas-positioning` for buyer value, `subscription-lifecycle` for renewals.
